\section{Blocklist}
\label{app:blocklist}

The following stemmed bigrams are blocked from the TF-IDF feature
matrix to prevent identity leakage and institutional boilerplate
from acting as spurious predictors. The blocklist contains
\textbf{94 bigrams in total}, organised into three thematic groups.

The blocklist was developed iteratively. The institutional-boilerplate
group was the largest contributor: an early version of the model
placed Reserve Bank city-pair bigrams (``cleveland richmond,''
``philadelphia cleveland,'' etc.) and standard FOMC roll-call
boilerplate (``board approv,'' ``governor approv,'' ``request
submit'') in the top features by Gini importance, but these are
pure leakage: they reflect the institutional structure of the
document rather than its policy stance, and a model classifying
decisions on ``cleveland richmond'' is exploiting the geographic
ordering of the dissent line rather than any economic content. The
personal-names group prevents the model from learning
chair-specific patterns, which are confounded with the era of each
chair's tenure (Greenspan with the Great Moderation, Bernanke with
the GFC, etc.). Removing these names forces the model to predict
from policy-relevant language rather than from chair identity.

pipeline and stored in \texttt{confusion\_matrices\_long\_v31.csv}.
The diagonal cells correspond to correct classifications

the proportion of observations on the diagonal, and the balanced
accuracy is the mean of the three per-class recall rates.

\section{Top Hawkish and Dovish Bigrams by Corpus}
\label{app:rf_features}

The Random Forest's top variable-importance features
(per-corpus, ranked by Gini importance times direction) were
extracted from \texttt{rf\_feature\_importance\_v31.csv} for each
of the four corpora. The full list is too long to reproduce
inline; the top-25 features per corpus are summarised
qualitatively in Section~\ref{sec:res_rf} of the main text.
The dominant patterns are:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=*]
  \item[FOMC statements:] Hawkish-leaning bigrams centre on
    explicit policy-action language (``rais target,'' ``increas
    target,'' ``ongo increas''), inflation-overshoot descriptors,
    and tightening-cycle vocabulary (``further increas'').
    Dovish-leaning bigrams centre on accommodation language
    (``maintain accommod,'' ``polici accommod''), crisis
    terminology (``zero lower,'' ``effect lower''), and
    downside-risk markers (``downsid risk'').
  \item[FOMC press conferences:] More diverse top features
    reflecting the wider vocabulary of the Q\&A. Hawkish features
    include policy-action bigrams (``raise rate,'' ``rate
    increas''), inflation framing (``price pressur,'' ``higher
    inflat''), and forward-guidance language. Dovish features
    include explicit easing language (``cut rate,'' ``rate cut''),
    employment weakness (``labor market''), and conditionality
    (``data depend'').
  \item[ECB statements:] Top hawkish features include
    ``rate increas,'' ``key rate'' (often in tightening-decision
    contexts), and inflation-target overshoot phrasings
    (``return inflat,'' ``well above''). Dovish features include
    ``rate cut,'' ``deposit facil'' (the deposit-facility-rate cuts
    of 2014--2016 and 2024--2025), and accommodation language.
  \item[ECB press conferences:] Largest feature space and richest
    variable-importance distribution. Hawkish features include
    inflation language (``second-round,'' ``broad-bas,'' ``wage
    growth'') and policy-action bigrams. Dovish features include
    sovereign-debt-crisis vocabulary (``transmiss mechan,''
    ``sovereign yield''), forward-guidance commitment (``stand
    readi''), and accommodation phrases.
\end{description}

The full top-25 lists for each corpus can be reconstructed by
filtering \texttt{rf\_feature\_importance\_v31.csv} on the
\texttt{corpus} column and sorting by the product of
\texttt{MeanDecreaseGini} and the absolute value of
\texttt{direction}. The qualitative observation that 40--60\% of
the top RF features overlap with the hand-crafted dictionary is
robust across corpora and is the empirical anchor for the
``overlapping but not identical'' interpretation in
Section~\ref{sec:res_horserace}.


\section{Lasso Feature Selection Details}
\label{app:lasso}

The Lasso pre-screening step uses an $\ell_1$-penalised multinomial
logit (for the RQ1 classification RF) or Gaussian-family Lasso (for
the RQ2 continuous-target RF) fit on the full TF-IDF matrix (after
blocklist), with the penalty parameter chosen by 10-fold
cross-validation at the 1-standard-error rule
(\texttt{lambda.1se}). If fewer than 5 features survive at
$\lambda_{\text{1se}}$, $\lambda_{\min}$ is used. If still fewer
than 5 features survive, the pipeline falls back to the 200 most
frequent bigrams in the training set, allowing the Random Forest to
perform implicit feature selection through its splitting criterion.
The features retained are the union of features with non-zero
coefficients across the three classes (cut, hold, hike).

The selection ratio is typically highest for the shorter-document
corpora (ECB statements) and lowest for the press-conference
corpora, which start from a much larger feature space (up to the
2{,}000-bigram cap on the TF-IDF vocabulary). The
feature-to-document ratio after Lasso is well below 1:1 in every
corpus, substantially mitigating the overfitting risk that motivated
the pre-screening step.
