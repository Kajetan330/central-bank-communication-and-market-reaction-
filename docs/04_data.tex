central bank text corpus (Section~\ref{sec:data_text}), the text preprocessing and

\section{Textual Corpus}
\label{sec:data_text}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The textual corpus consists of \textbf{884 central bank communication
documents} from the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) and the
European Central Bank (ECB). For each institution, two document types are
analysed: policy statements and press conference transcripts.
\Cref{tab:corpus_overview} summarises the final corpus, while
\Cref{fig:sample_coverage} illustrates the coverage of each document
type over time.



The raw corpus is constructed from four pre-compiled files:
\texttt{fomc\_statements\_econometrics.csv},
\texttt{fomc\_press\_conferences\_econometrics.csv},
\texttt{ecb\_decisions\_econometrics.csv}, and
\texttt{ecb\_statements\_econometrics.csv}. These files contain the raw
text, meeting date, central bank identifier, document type, and source
information. After loading, all documents are combined into a single
dataset and then split into four analysis corpora: FOMC statements, FOMC
press conferences, ECB statements, and ECB press conferences.

\subsection{FOMC Statements}
\label{sec:data_fomc_stmt}

FOMC post-meeting statements are collected from February~1994, when the
Committee first began issuing post-meeting communications, through
March~2026. The final FOMC statement corpus contains
\textbf{242 documents}, including both scheduled meetings and
inter-meeting communications. These documents are official committee
statements and therefore do not require speaker-turn cleaning or removal
of journalist questions.

The statements are matched to the corresponding FOMC event dates in the
U.S. Monetary Policy Event-Study Database. This allows each statement to
be linked to high-frequency market reactions in the statement window,
including monetary policy surprises, equity-market reactions,
exchange-rate movements, and Treasury yield changes. Before scoring, the
statement texts are processed through the common cleaning and
tokenisation pipeline described in \Cref{sec:data_tokenisation},
including lowercasing, stop-word handling, phrase matching, and stemming.

\subsection{FOMC Press Conferences}
\label{sec:data_fomc_pc}

FOMC press conference transcripts are available from April~2011 onward,
yielding \textbf{92 documents}. Press conferences were initially held
after selected meetings, typically those associated with the Summary of
Economic Projections, and have been held after every scheduled FOMC
meeting since January~2019. The transcripts are sourced from
\texttt{fomc\_press\_conferences\_econometrics.csv} and include the
Chair's prepared remarks as well as the subsequent question-and-answer
session.

Because the raw transcripts contain both central bank communication and
journalist questions, they are cleaned before scoring. This step is
important because retaining journalist questions would contaminate the
tone measure with language that does not originate from the central bank.
The final FOMC press conference corpus therefore retains only the Chair's
opening remarks and answers, while journalist questions and other
non-Chair speaker turns are removed. The cleaning procedure is described
in \Cref{sec:data_fomc_pc_clean}.

\subsection{ECB Statements}
\label{sec:data_ecb_stmt}

ECB statements refer to the introductory monetary policy statement
delivered by the ECB President at the start of the press conference.
They are collected for Governing Council monetary policy meetings from
January~1999 through March~2026, yielding \textbf{284 documents}. The
statement corpus is sourced from \texttt{ecb\_decisions\_econometrics.csv}
and forms the ECB statement sample used in the empirical analysis.

The ECB held monetary policy meetings monthly until January~2015, which
creates a denser early sample than for the FOMC. After the ECB switched
to a six-weekly monetary policy meeting schedule, the observation
frequency became closer to that of the FOMC.

\subsection{ECB Press Conferences}
\label{sec:data_ecb_pc}

ECB press conferences capture the question-and-answer part of the
President's monetary policy press conference. The corpus contains
\textbf{266 documents} from January~1999 through December~2025 and is
sourced from \texttt{ecb\_statements\_econometrics.csv}. The sample is
smaller than the ECB statement corpus because Q\&A transcripts are not
available in usable form for every policy meeting.

As with the FOMC press conferences, the ECB transcripts require cleaning
because the raw text contains both journalist questions and central bank
answers. The analysis retains only the responses by ECB officials and
removes journalist questions. This ensures that the measured tone
reflects ECB communication rather than the wording or framing of the
questions. The cleaning procedure is described in
\Cref{sec:data_ecb_pc_clean}.

\section{Text Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data_preprocessing}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The preprocessing pipeline has two purposes. First, it removes text that does not
represent central bank communication, especially journalist questions and
institutional boilerplate.(Need to mention that otherwise it would bias results) Second, it converts the remaining text into a form suitable
for dictionary scoring and machine-learning feature extraction. Statements require no
speaker-turn cleaning, while press conference transcripts require more extensive
processing because they contain multiple speakers.

\subsection{ECB Press Conference Cleaning}
\label{sec:data_ecb_pc_clean}

ECB press conference transcripts contain both official ECB communication
and journalist questions. Since journalist questions do not represent the
ECB's policy stance, they are removed before scoring. The cleaned text
retains only identifiable ECB speaker responses, including those by the
President, Vice-President, Chief Economist, and other senior ECB
officials.

The cleaning procedure removes institutional boilerplate, detects
speaker turns, discards question segments, and concatenates the official
ECB responses into a single cleaned document. This ensures that the
press-conference tone measure reflects ECB communication rather than the
language or framing of journalists' questions.

\Cref{tab:ecb_pc_cleaning} reports diagnostics from the cleaning
procedure. Documents with unusually high or low text retention are flagged
for inspection, since very high retention may indicate that journalist
questions were not fully removed, while very low retention may indicate
over-cleaning.

\subsection{FOMC Press Conference Cleaning}
\label{sec:data_fomc_pc_clean}

FOMC press conference transcripts contain both the Chair's communication
and journalist questions. Since the objective is to measure the central
bank's tone, the cleaning procedure retains only the Chair's prepared
remarks and answers, while removing journalist questions and other
speaker turns.

This step prevents the tone score from being affected by the wording of
questions, which may contain market interpretations, criticism, or
hypothetical policy scenarios that do not represent the Federal Reserve's
own communication. The cleaned FOMC press conference corpus is therefore
designed to capture only the Chair's policy-relevant language.

\subsection{Cleaning Validation}
\label{sec:data_cleaning_validation}

Press conference cleaning can affect measured tone because journalist
questions may contain hawkish or dovish language that does not represent
the central bank's own communication. To assess the effect of cleaning,
dictionary scores are computed both before and after removing journalist
questions. The comparison is based on the final dictionary score,
\[
  \texttt{net\_score} = \frac{H-D}{H+D+10},
\]
where \(H\) and \(D\) denote the total number of hawkish and dovish
phrase matches. Scores above \(0.05\) are classified as hawkish, scores
below \(-0.05\) as dovish, and scores between these thresholds as
neutral.

The validation compares raw and cleaned scores to check whether the
cleaning procedure materially changes the tone measure. In particular,
it examines whether cleaned scores remain closely related to raw scores,
whether cleaning shifts the average score in a systematic direction, and
whether many documents change their hawkish/neutral/dovish classification
after cleaning.

A remaining limitation is that removing journalist questions does not
fully eliminate question-related language. Central bankers sometimes
repeat or paraphrase a question before answering it. In such cases, the
repeated wording remains in the cleaned text because it is part of the
central banker's response. The resulting score should therefore be
interpreted as the tone of the official response text, not as a perfect
separation between original central bank language and all question-related
phrasing.

The validation confirms that cleaning does not fundamentally alter the
tone measure, but it can affect the classification of individual
documents. All main empirical results therefore use the cleaned
press-conference texts, since these are designed to measure central bank
communication rather than the full transcript including journalist
questions.

\subsection{Tokenisation and Stemming}
\label{sec:data_tokenisation}

After cleaning, all texts pass through a common preprocessing pipeline.
Text is lowercased and converted into phrase-level features. The
dictionary scorer uses bigrams and trigrams, while the Random Forest
pipeline uses bigram TF-IDF features. Words are stemmed using the Porter
stemming algorithm implemented in the \texttt{SnowballC} package
\citep{porter1980}. For example, ``raising'', ``raised'', and ``raises''
are all mapped to the stem ``rais''. The same stemming logic is applied
to both document text and dictionary terms, ensuring that inflected word
forms are matched consistently.

The dictionary scorer uses a conservative custom stop-word list. This
list removes only non-directional function words, such as articles,
auxiliary verbs, pronouns, and basic prepositions. Directional and
stance-relevant words such as ``above'', ``below'', ``further'', ``not'',
and ``more'' are deliberately retained, because they are central to
monetary-policy meaning. For example, removing ``above'' from
``above target'' or ``further'' from ``further tightening'' would destroy
the stance signal. Dictionary phrases are therefore preprocessed using
the same stop-word and stemming rules as the documents before matching.

The Random Forest pipeline uses a separate preprocessing approach. It
constructs stemmed bigrams from the cleaned text, removes bigrams that
contain standard stop words, and converts the remaining terms into a
TF-IDF matrix. Terms that appear in fewer than three documents are
excluded, and the feature space is capped at 2{,}000 bigrams for
computational efficiency and to reduce overfitting. A manually defined
blocklist removes institutional names, locations, and boilerplate terms
that are frequent but not informative about hawkish or dovish tone.

In the final dictionary score, each document is scored using matched
hawkish and dovish bigram/trigram phrases. The Random Forest instead
uses the document's bigram TF-IDF representation as input to the Lasso
feature-selection and classification pipeline described in
\Cref{sec:meth_rf}.

\subsection{Sentence-Level RoBERTa/BERT Predictions}
\label{sec:data_bert_predictions}

In addition to the dictionary and Random Forest inputs, the final dataset
includes sentence-level RoBERTa/BERT predictions for each of the four
corpora: FOMC statements, FOMC press conferences, ECB statements, and ECB
press conferences. Each sentence is classified as hawkish, neutral, or
dovish. These sentence-level classifications are then aggregated to the
document level to construct the \bertscore{} used in the empirical
analysis.

The RoBERTa/BERT measure provides a contextual benchmark against the
dictionary and Random Forest scores. Unlike the dictionary, which relies
on matched bigrams and trigrams, the sentence classifier evaluates the
broader sentence context. This is useful because the meaning of a phrase
can depend on surrounding words. For example, a dictionary may treat a
phrase such as ``inflation pressures'' similarly across documents, while
a sentence-level model can distinguish whether the sentence describes
rising pressures, easing pressures, or uncertainty about future
pressures.

The RoBERTa/BERT scores are not treated as manually verified ground-truth
labels. They are used as an additional text-based tone measure that may
capture contextual information missed by fixed phrase counts. This
distinction is important because sentence-level classifications may not
map perfectly into monetary-policy stance, especially when the same model
is applied across both FOMC and ECB communication.

\section{Financial Market Data}
\label{sec:data_market}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The market-reaction analysis uses high-frequency asset-price changes
measured in narrow windows around monetary policy announcements. These
windows are designed to isolate the market response to central bank
communication and reduce the likelihood that unrelated news drives the
measured price movement. The analysis relies on two purpose-built
monetary policy event-study databases: the U.S. Monetary Policy
Event-Study Database for the FOMC and the Euro Area Monetary Policy
Database for the ECB.

\subsection{US Monetary Policy Database (USMPD)}
\label{sec:data_usmpd}

Intraday financial market reactions to FOMC announcements are obtained
from the U.S. Monetary Policy Event-Study Database of
\citet{acosta2025}.\footnote{The database is accessed through
\texttt{USMPD.xlsx}, using the separate sheets for statement and press
conference event windows.} The database reports separate high-frequency
windows for the statement release and the press conference. This
separation is important for the empirical design because it allows FOMC
statements and FOMC press conferences to be analysed as distinct
communication channels rather than as a single daily monetary policy
event.

For each FOMC event, the textual tone measures are matched to the
corresponding market-reaction window. Statement scores are matched to the
statement window, while press-conference scores are matched to the
press-conference window. The resulting dependent variables capture
high-frequency changes in equity prices, exchange rates, and Treasury
yields around the relevant communication event.

The following variables are extracted:
 
\begin{description}[noitemsep, leftmargin=2.7cm, font=\normalfont\bfseries]
  \item[MP1] The target-rate surprise, based on the current-month
    federal funds futures contract and scaled to reflect the implied
    surprise in the current policy decision \citep{kuttner2001}. MP1
    is the main control variable in FOMC regressions and is reported
    in basis points.
  \item[S\&P~500] The event-window return on the S\&P~500 equity
    index, measured in percent.
  \item[EUR/USD] The event-window change in the EUR/USD exchange
    rate, measured in percent. An increase denotes euro appreciation
    and dollar depreciation.
  \item[DXY] The event-window change in the U.S. Dollar Index, used
    as a robustness measure for dollar movements. The expected sign
    is opposite to EUR/USD: FOMC hawkishness should increase DXY.
  \item[UST~2Y, 5Y, 10Y, 30Y] Event-window changes in U.S.
    Treasury yields at the 2-year, 5-year, 10-year, and 30-year
    maturities, reported in basis points.
  \item[TIPS~5Y, TIPS~10Y] Event-window changes in 5-year and
    10-year Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS)
    breakeven inflation rates, reported in basis points. TIPS
    breakevens measure market-implied inflation compensation and
    provide a channel test: under the standard monetary channel,
    hawkish tone should reduce breakevens, while under the
    information effect of \citet{nakamura2018}, hawkish tone
    signalling economic strength could raise them.
\end{description}

The resulting dependent variables capture high-frequency changes in
equity prices, exchange rates, and Treasury yields around the relevant
communication event. For FOMC statements, the main control variable is
the statement-window monetary policy surprise, denoted \texttt{MP1}.

The USMPD is merged with the FOMC text data by meeting date. Statements
are matched to the statement-window data, while press conferences are
matched to the press-conference-window data, ensuring that each document
is linked to the relevant high-frequency market reaction.

\subsection{Euro Area Monetary Policy Database (EA-MPD)}
\label{sec:data_eampd}

Market reactions to ECB announcements are obtained from the Euro Area
Monetary Policy Database of \citet{altavilla2019}.\footnote{The database
is accessed through \texttt{EA-MPD.xlsx}, using the press release and
press conference window sheets.} The EA-MPD provides separate
high-frequency event windows for the policy announcement and the press
conference. This distinction is important for the empirical design,
because ECB statements can be matched to the press release window, while
ECB press conferences can be matched to the press conference window.

This allows the analysis to estimate whether the two ECB communication
channels contain different market-relevant information, rather than
treating the full announcement day as one combined event.

The following variables are extracted:
 
\begin{description}[noitemsep, leftmargin=2.7cm, font=\normalfont\bfseries]
  \item[OIS\_MP1] The short-maturity OIS surprise used as the ECB
    analogue to MP1. The exact source column is detected by the
    pipeline because EA-MPD column names vary across releases. The
    variable is interpreted as the unexpected component of the
    current policy decision and is measured in basis points.
  \item[SX5E] The event-window return on the Euro~Stoxx~50 index,
    measured in percent.
  \item[SX7E] The event-window return on the Euro~Stoxx Banks index,
    capturing the banking channel of monetary policy transmission in
    the euro area. Euro area banks are particularly sensitive to ECB
    rate decisions due to the direct effect on net interest margins.
  \item[EUR/USD] The event-window change in EUR/USD, measured in
    percent. The sign convention is the same as for the FOMC
    regressions: an increase denotes euro appreciation. The expected
    sign differs by central bank: FOMC hawkishness should reduce
    EUR/USD through dollar appreciation, while ECB hawkishness should
    increase EUR/USD through euro appreciation.
  \item[OIS~2Y, 5Y, 10Y] Event-window changes in euro area OIS
    rates at the 2-year, 5-year, and 10-year maturities, measured
    in basis points.
  \item[DE~2Y, DE~10Y] Event-window changes in German Bund yields
    at the 2-year and 10-year maturities, measured in basis points.
    German Bunds serve as the risk-free benchmark for the euro area.
  \item[IT10Y\_spread] The event-window change in the Italian BTP
    10-year yield minus the German Bund 10-year yield, measured in
    basis points. This sovereign spread captures the financial
    fragmentation channel that is specific to ECB communication,
    particularly during the sovereign debt crisis period. An increase
    denotes widening of the Italian premium relative to Germany.
\end{description}
 
The baseline analysis uses only the press release and press conference
windows, because these correspond directly to the two ECB text corpora.

\section{Interest Rate Decisions}
\label{sec:data_rates}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Interest rate decisions are hand-coded for FOMC meetings from
February~1994 to March~2026 and ECB Governing Council monetary policy
meetings from January~1999 to March~2026. Each decision is recorded as
the change in the relevant policy rate in basis points, denoted
\bpschange{}. A positive value indicates a rate hike, zero indicates a
hold, and a negative value indicates a rate cut.

The decision variable is used in three ways. First, it provides a
benchmark for the agreement analysis in \Cref{sec:res_agreement_new}. A
hawkish text label is treated as consistent with a hike, a dovish label
as consistent with a cut, and a neutral label as consistent with an
unchanged rate. This benchmark is useful but imperfect. There is no
direct observable ground-truth label for whether a communication event
was hawkish, neutral, or dovish. Rate changes are therefore used as a
proxy, even though a hold may still be hawkish if it signals future
tightening, and a hike may be interpreted as dovish if it was smaller
than markets expected. Agreement results should therefore be interpreted
as alignment with realised policy actions, not as definitive validation
of the true communication tone.

This distinction is especially important because realised policy stance
and market-surprise stance are not the same object. A realised rate hike
is hawkish relative to an unchanged policy rate, but it is not necessarily
hawkish from the market's perspective. If markets expected exactly the
realised hike, the rate decision is market-neutral. If markets expected a
larger hike, the same realised hike may be interpreted as dovish. If
markets expected a smaller hike or no hike, it is hawkish.

The market-surprise benchmark is therefore based on high-frequency
surprise measures rather than on the realised rate change:
\[
M_t =
\begin{cases}
\text{hawkish}, & S_t > \kappa,\\
\text{neutral}, & |S_t| \leq \kappa,\\
\text{dovish},  & S_t < -\kappa,
\end{cases}
\]
where \(S_t\) is the relevant event-window surprise and \(\kappa\) is a
small neutral threshold. For statement and press-release windows,
\(S_t\) is based on the current-rate surprise: \(MP1_t\) for the FOMC
and \(OIS\_MP1_t\) for the ECB. For press conferences, the rate decision
has already been released, so expectation revisions are more naturally
captured by path or yield-window surprises, such as \(MP2_t\),
\(OIS\_MP2_t\), or two-year yield changes.

The distribution of rate decisions is highly imbalanced. Holds account

performing poorly on the cut and hike classes that are most relevant for

For classification, the exact magnitude of the rate change is collapsed
into three classes. Negative changes are coded as cuts, zero changes as
holds, and positive changes as hikes. This means that both standard
25-basis-point moves and smaller ECB changes during the low-rate period
enter the same directional classification. For the forecasting analysis
in RQ2, however, the rate change is kept as a continuous basis-point
variable, so the magnitude of the decision is preserved.

\subsection{Propagation to Press Conferences}
\label{sec:data_rate_propagation}

Rate decisions are coded at the meeting level. Since the press conference
belongs to the same monetary policy event as the statement, the same
\bpschange{} value is assigned to the corresponding press conference by
meeting date. This allows press conference documents to be included in
the agreement analysis and in the Random Forest classification task.


\subsection{Forecast Targets for RQ2}
\label{sec:data_rate_rq2}

For RQ2, forecast targets are constructed separately within each of the
four corpora: FOMC statements, FOMC press conferences, ECB statements,
and ECB press conferences. Observations are first sorted chronologically,
and lead variables are then created within each corpus. This ensures
that next-meeting targets are not pooled across institutions or document
types.

The forecasting analysis uses four targets. The variables
\texttt{bps\_next} and \texttt{bps\_next2} denote the policy-rate change
at the next meeting and two meetings ahead. The variables
\texttt{y2\_next} and \texttt{y2\_next2} denote the corresponding
one-meeting-ahead and two-meeting-ahead changes in the two-year
rate-expectation outcome. For the FOMC, this is based on the two-year
Treasury yield reaction. For the ECB, it is based on the two-year OIS
reaction.

The benchmark control for rate-decision forecasts is the current
policy-rate change, \texttt{bps\_curr}. For two-year yield forecasts, the
corresponding benchmark is the current two-year yield reaction,
\texttt{y2\_curr}. Because lead variables are used, the final observation
in each corpus has no one-meeting-ahead target, and the final two
observations have no two-meeting-ahead target.

The expanding-window design further reduces the effective sample. The
first 30 observations in each corpus are used as the initial training
window, and the first forecast is produced for observation 31. At each
subsequent step, the training sample expands by one observation, so only
information available before the forecasted meeting is used.

\section{Sample Construction and Attrition}
\label{sec:data_sample}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The final regression samples are constructed by merging the scored text
documents with the corresponding high-frequency market data and
rate-decision variables. Each specification is then estimated on the
available observations with non-missing values for the relevant tone
measure, market outcome, and monetary policy surprise control. As a
result, the effective sample size differs across outcomes and methods.

Attrition mainly arises from missing market-window observations and, in
some cases, missing tone scores after text cleaning or model construction.
This is particularly relevant for press conference samples, where the
availability of transcripts and separate event-window data is more limited
than for statement samples.

For each corpus, the baseline market-reaction sample is defined by the
intersection of three elements: a valid text score, a matched
high-frequency market reaction, and a non-missing policy surprise control
(\texttt{MP1} for FOMC events and \texttt{OIS\_MP1} for ECB events).
The exact number of observations therefore varies by dependent variable
and is reported in the regression tables.

\begin{description}[noitemsep]
  \item[Naive sample:] Requires non-missing \texttt{net\_score}, equity return, and
  EUR/USD return. This sample is used for regressions without the rate-surprise control.
  \item[Controlled sample:] Additionally requires a non-missing MP1 or OIS\_MP1
  rate-surprise control. This is the main RQ1 regression sample.
\end{description}

results tables.

corpus-specific datasets: FOMC statements, FOMC press conferences, ECB
statements, and ECB press conferences. Each dataset contains the cleaned
text, dictionary scores, Random Forest scores, RoBERTa/BERT scores,
rate-decision variables, forecast targets, and matched high-frequency
market reactions. This structure is maintained throughout the empirical
analysis so that statements and press conferences are evaluated as
distinct communication channels rather than mechanically pooled.
